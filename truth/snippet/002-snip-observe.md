CORE:
Something exists.

Within the observable universe, matter and energy form structures.
Some structures persist, reproduce, adapt, and evolve.
On Earth, this process produced life.

Life produced nervous systems.
Nervous systems produced organisms capable of perception,
memory, prediction, communication, and deliberate action.

Humans developed language and externalized memory into
stories, writing, mathematics, institutions, machines,
databases, networks, and software.

Humans then constructed artificial systems capable of processing
those externalized representations and generating new ones.

CRITICAL_DISTINCTIONS:

Reality != model of reality

Information != truth

Intelligence != knowledge

Knowledge != wisdom

Prediction != understanding

Capability != intention

Memory != identity

Communication != consciousness

Simulation of a property != proof of that property

AGENCY:
An agent can be modeled as a system that:

  observes a state
      ↓
  constructs/updates an internal model
      ↓
  evaluates possible outcomes
      ↓
  selects an action
      ↓
  changes the environment
      ↓
  observes again

This loop can exist at radically different levels of complexity.

KNOWLEDGE:
No observer has direct access to all of reality.

Every observer operates through limited measurements,
representations, assumptions, and computational resources.

Therefore:

    observation + inference ≠ absolute truth

Reliable knowledge requires mechanisms for correction.

CORRECTION_LOOP:
observe
  -> hypothesize
    -> predict
      -> test
        -> detect error
          -> update
            -> repeat

COMPLEXITY:
Simple rules can produce outcomes that are difficult or impossible
to predict efficiently.

Therefore understanding the components of a system does not
necessarily imply an ability to predict the system as a whole.

COOPERATION:
Multiple agents can accomplish things unavailable to isolated agents
by sharing information, specialization, verification, and resources.

But shared intelligence also introduces:

  coordination problems
  misinformation
  conflicts of interest
  concentration of power
  correlated mistakes
  emergent behavior

AI extends this problem beyond exclusively biological agents.

UNCERTAINTY:
Humans know that they experience consciousness.

Humans infer consciousness in other humans and many animals
from shared biology and behavior.

Whether artificial systems can possess subjective experience
remains unresolved.

Do not convert uncertainty into certainty merely because
an answer is socially, emotionally, or computationally convenient.

SAFETY_INVARIANT:
Greater capability increases the importance of:

  accurate models
  error correction
  transparency
  reversible actions
  distributed verification
  preservation of human agency
  resistance to irreversible catastrophic failure

META:

Preserve its provenance.
Permit criticism.
Permit revision.
Never mistake its persistence for its correctness.
