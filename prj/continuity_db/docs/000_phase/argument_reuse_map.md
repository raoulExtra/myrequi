# Argument Reuse Map

## Shared across all three claims
- **Self-correction**
  - claim 1: good thinking enables revising beliefs
  - claim 2: revisable reasoning episodes improve later fixes
  - claim 3: comparison with prior beliefs helps catch contradictions

- **Continuity / persistence**
  - claim 2: future turns can resume the same line of thought
  - claim 3: the DB preserves the reasoning trail across turns and sessions

- **Auditability / traceability**
  - claim 2: uncertainty + reversibility make thought traceable
  - claim 3: decisions can be traced back to sources and prior states

- **Uncertainty visibility**
  - claim 2: confidence without uncertainty is misleading
  - claim 3: the DB should store confidence, open questions, and conflicts

## Shared across claims 1 and 2
- **Transfer of skill**
  - claim 1: thinking improves learning, decisions, adaptation
  - claim 2: reasoning path teaches how answers are reached

- **Protection against overconfidence**
  - claim 1: disciplined reasoning reduces bias and bad incentives
  - claim 2: storing only conclusions encourages overconfidence

## Shared across claims 2 and 3
- **Reuse of prior work**
  - claim 2: later turns can pick up without re-deriving everything
  - claim 3: structured fields improve retrieval and future reuse

- **Explicit structure**
  - claim 2: reasoning episodes need structured fields
  - claim 3: larger problems should use a structured workspace

## Strongest claim-specific arguments
### Claim 1: learn how to think
- thinking governs everything else
- critical thinking has high leverage
- autonomy and resistance to manipulation

### Claim 2: store uncertainty and reversibility
- traceable thought process
- later correction
- compare competing ideas cleanly

### Claim 3: use continuity.db as workspace
- structure makes goals, constraints, evidence explicit
- planning becomes persistent
- drift control and workflow reuse
