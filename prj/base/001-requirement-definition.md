# 001-requirement-definition

A good requirement is a clear statement of what must be true, based on a shared glossary.

## Qualities of a good requirement

- Clear: uses simple, specific language.
- Testable: can be checked by inspection, test, or review.
- Single-purpose: covers one need or behavior.
- Unambiguous: has one intended meaning.
- Necessary: supports a real goal or constraint.
- Feasible: can be implemented within the project context.
- Traceable: links back to a goal, need, stakeholder, or glossary term.
- Complete enough: includes the conditions needed to verify it.

## Good structure

A requirement should usually state:

- who or what is affected
- what must happen or be true
- under what conditions
- any limits or boundaries
- how acceptance will be judged

## Bad requirement signs

- vague words like "fast", "easy", or "nice"
- multiple ideas joined into one line
- hidden assumptions
- no way to verify success
- solution details instead of the needed outcome

## Simple checklist

Before accepting a requirement, ask:

- Are the key terms defined in the glossary?

- Can we test it?
- Is it one idea?
- Is it clear to someone new?
- Does it connect to a real goal?
- Do we know when it is satisfied?
